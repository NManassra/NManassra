# AMAN — Graduation Project

**Trauma-informed, Arabic audio-first digital support for children living under continuous exposure in Gaza**

AMAN is my Computer Engineering graduation project at Birzeit University. It explores how a digital system can prioritize emotional regulation, accessibility, autonomy, and safe interaction when children are living through ongoing trauma rather than a stable post-trauma period.

## What I worked on

- Built the mobile experience with **Flutter and Dart**
- Integrated the application with **Python backend services and APIs**
- Designed Arabic audio-first flows to reduce reading and interaction barriers
- Supported gentle, optional interaction through voice, drawing, and symbolic expression
- Applied safety-centered UX principles that avoid forced narration and aggressive visual cues
- Contributed to end-to-end integration, testing, and verification

## Core design principles

1. **Regulation before narration** — stabilization and grounding come before asking a child to describe difficult experiences.
2. **Audio-first accessibility** — Arabic voice guidance helps children interact when reading, attention, or connectivity is difficult.
3. **Avatar-based conversational interaction** — a gentle interface supports optional voice, drawing, or symbolic responses.
4. **Emotionally safe interaction** — the system preserves autonomy and avoids pressure, judgment, or forced disclosure.

## Technology

`Flutter` · `Dart` · `Python` · `REST APIs` · `AI-assisted interaction` · `Arabic UX` · `Audio-first design`

## System overview

The Flutter application delivers the child-facing experience and communicates with Python services through APIs. The interaction layer coordinates Arabic audio guidance, avatar-led activities, and optional expression modes while keeping safety and user control central to the flow.
